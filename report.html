The Acquired Portfolio

Every episode of Acquired opens with the same disclaimer: "this is not investment advice." What if it were? Starting in October 2015, we bought one share of every publicly traded company the main feed has covered — at the close, on the day the episode aired — and held.

Methodology

Rules of the backtest

  • Main feed only — no ACQ2, Arena Show, or specials.
  • One share, bought at the close on the air date; weekend/holiday air dates buy at the next close.
  • Multi-part series count each part as its own episode and buy again.
  • Public subject → buy the subject's ticker. Already-acquired subject → buy the public parent, and sell on any later divestiture.
  • Private subject with no public parent → skipped, but counted and reported.
  • Non-US subjects → a US ADR where one exists, otherwise the primary listing converted to USD.
  • Dividends reinvested (total return) and splits tracked; cash buyouts move to cash at the deal price, held at 0% thereafter; stock buyouts convert at the exchange ratio.
  • Benchmark is the same dollars, on the same dates, into the S&P 500 total return index. Metric is money-weighted XIRR. No transaction costs, taxes, or interest on cash.

Data caveats

  • 9 delisted tickers — VA, LNKD, YHOO, WFM, ATVI, EA, WORK, EB, TWTR — are valued from hand-researched, sparse closing prices, linearly interpolated between known dates. Flagged "manual" in the table.
  • Cash from a buyout sits at 0% for the rest of the backtest — no money-market yield modeled.
  • One share means one share: results are dominated by whichever stock had the highest price on its air date — GOOGL pre-split (~$750–1,160), AMZN pre-split (~$650–1,330), BKNG (~$1,975). That's a share-count artifact, not a stock-picking signal.
  • No taxes, fees, or transaction costs modeled anywhere.
  • The Sony lot excludes the Sony Financial Group spin-off distributed to ADR holders in October 2025 (roughly 4% of that position). Hand-priced delisted tickers ignore dividends.
  • Independently reviewed: an adversarial pass re-verified all corporate actions, the delisted-ticker prices, the benchmark, and the XIRR, and caught a cost-basis bug that has since been fixed. Details in the project's review notes.
  • This is a backtest of a joke. Please do not buy one share of everything a podcast mentions. Not investment advice.
